# USDA-NIFA AFRI Proposal Outline
## Productivity and Sustainability of Soybean Deficit Irrigation in the US

### I. OVERVIEW & PROPOSAL NARRATIVE STRUCTURE

#### A. Overview Section (2-3 pages)
- Problem statement: Water scarcity in agricultural regions
- Importance of soybean production in the US
- Deficit irrigation as a solution
- Innovation: Integration of field experiments + process-based modeling
- Expected outcomes and impact

#### B. Rationale and Significance
- Current state of soybean production and water use
- Climate change and irrigation challenges
- Knowledge gaps in deficit irrigation response
- Why this research matters to USDA/farmers/environment

#### C. Research Objectives and Approach
**Objective 1: Field Experiment**
- Conduct multi-location field experiments on deficit irrigation in soybeans
- Measure yield, water use efficiency, soil moisture
- 2-3 growing seasons

**Objective 2: Model Improvement #1 - Soil Water Dynamics**
- Enhance process-based model for soil water movement under deficit stress
- Integrate deficit irrigation parameters into existing models

**Objective 3: Model Improvement #2 - Crop Physiology**
- Improve representation of soybean physiological response to water deficit
- Calibrate with field data

**Objective 4: Model Application #1 - Scenario Analysis**
- Use models to simulate climate scenarios and irrigation strategies
- Generate decision-support tools for farmers

**Objective 5: Model Application #2 - Regional Assessment**
- Assess soybean production vulnerability across major US soybean regions
- Identify high-risk areas and adaptation strategies

**Objective 6: Model Application #3 - Policy Implications**
- Develop recommendations for water management policies
- Cost-benefit analysis of deficit irrigation adoption

#### D. Expected Outcomes
- 4-6 peer-reviewed publications
- 1-2 software tools/decision support systems
- Extension bulletins and farmer guides
- Trained personnel (students, postdocs)

#### E. Significance & Innovation
- Novelty: Integrated field-modeling approach for US soybean regions
- Broad applicability across US agricultural regions
- Addresses USDA priorities: productivity, sustainability, water
- Farmer adoption potential

### III. KEY RESEARCH QUESTIONS
1. How do soybean yields and quality respond to deficit irrigation across US regions?
2. Which physiological processes limit soybean production under water deficit?
3. How can we improve process-based models to better predict deficit irrigation effects?
4. What deficit irrigation strategies maximize productivity and sustainability?
5. Which US regions are most vulnerable to water scarcity?
6. What policies would facilitate adoption of deficit irrigation technologies?

### IV. INNOVATION & SIGNIFICANCE
- **Novelty:** First comprehensive field-modeling integration for US soybean deficit irrigation
- **Scale:** Multi-location field studies (3-5 locations across US soybean belt)
- **Impact:** Direct application for 40+ million acres of US soybean production
- **Climate Readiness:** Addresses impacts of climate change on water availability
- **Farmer Engagement:** Decision tools for on-farm implementation

### V. ALIGNMENT WITH AFRI PRIORITIES
- **Productivity:** Optimize yields under water stress
- **Sustainability:** Reduce water use while maintaining production
- **Climate:** Address climate change adaptation in irrigated agriculture
- **Competitiveness:** Help US farmers compete globally under water constraints
